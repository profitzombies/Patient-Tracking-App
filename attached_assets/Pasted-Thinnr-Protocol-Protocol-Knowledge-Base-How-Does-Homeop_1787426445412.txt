We should also not forget Adaptive Thermogenesis, which is an additional energy expenditure that is not included in dietary thermogenesis. Adaptive thermogenesis is manifested primarily as an increase in BMR due to environmental stresses, one of which is an alteration in traditional food intake. Additionally, think of this as an added "bonus" for some people... Overconsumption affects thermogenesis. When energy intake (calories) increases above normal levels needed for dietary thermogenesis and the regulation of body temperature. High-calorie grazing all day is imperative because a person who only consumes 3 meals per day will experience peaks and valleys in their metabolism. My patient reported having right-sided abdominal pain. What could it be, and what should I tell them?  Right-sided abdominal pain during the Thinnr Protocol can occur for several reasons and is not automatically caused by the protocol itself. One possible consideration is gallbladder involvement, particularly if the patient is experiencing faster-than-expected weight loss. During periods of rapid fat loss, the gallbladder may be under increased functional demand, which can contribute to discomfort in some individuals. However, abdominal pain can also be unrelated to the protocol, and proper information gathering is necessary before drawing conclusions. 
It is important to collect additional details, including what day of the protocol the patient is on, how much weight they have lost so far, whether their weight loss is within expected ranges, and the exact location of the pain (upper right vs. lower right abdomen). Upper right abdominal pain in combination with accelerated weight loss may increase the likelihood of gallbladder involvement, whereas pain occurring with normal or slower-than-expected weight loss makes gallbladder involvement less likely.
If the patient has also experienced dizziness, dehydration, and electrolyte imbalance should be considered, as these are relatively common during weight loss and can typically be improved with proper hydration and electrolyte support.
If abdominal pain is persistent, severe, worsening, or concerning, the patient should discontinue the protocol until further evaluation and consult their licensed healthcare provider to determine the underlying cause.

3) Are the foods that they are eating a good source of magnesium? Low magnesium is frequently related to slow bowel motility and constipation. Any good quality OTC magnesium supplement can be recommended.

5) Have they experienced either intermittent or chronic constipation previously in their life? If the patient has a pre-existing history of constipation, it is recommended that they start taking a sugar-free fiber supplement beginning on the first day of the Thinnr Metabolic Thinning Phase.

My patient says that they feel cold all the time or like they can’t get warm.  What should I tell them?  This is a somewhat common experience during the Thinnr Metabolic Activation phase. not burning as many calories, therefore not as much heat is being generated. 
The liver may not be producing as much alcohol as fatty liver infiltration is decreasing. The liver often becomes less steatotic while on the Thinnr Protocol. 
If the patient is in the maintenance phase: After losing a significant amount of weight, it’s very common for people to feel cold more often—even when they are no longer trying to lose weight. One of the biggest reasons is simply that there is less body fat. Body fat acts like insulation, so when you lose it, the body doesn’t hold heat as well as it used to. The same room temperature that once felt comfortable can suddenly feel chilly.
Another reason is that the body becomes more efficient after weight loss… lower weight means a lower BMR (calories burned), and a lower BMR means less internal heat production. This is a normal adjustment and doesn’t mean anything is wrong.
Hormone signals related to fat loss can also affect how warm a person feels. When body fat decreases, the signals that help regulate energy use and heat production also decrease. This can make a person feel colder, especially in the hands and feet.
Finally, people who have lost weight usually eat less food than they did before, even while maintaining. Digestion itself generates heat (dietary thermogenesis), so taking in fewer calories can reduce the warmth your body generates throughout the day. 
The good news is that this cold sensitivity is temporary and improves over time as the body fully adjusts to its new weight, eating patterns, and activity level.

1) The initiation of fasting, substantial changes in diet, and detoxification can disrupt the gut microbiome, leading to Candida overgrowth 
2) A Herxheimer reaction, which as when yeast is dying off it can trigger the “Herxheimer reaction,” which includes symptoms like fatigue, brain fog, and in some cases, an apparent flare-up of yeast symptoms (mouth or vaginal thrush) before things improve.
You can make your normal “yeast infection” recommendations for the patient, or, if you do not have a standard recommendation, consider recommending that they start taking a high CFU multi-strain probiotic.
