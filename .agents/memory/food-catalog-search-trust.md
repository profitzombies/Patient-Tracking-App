---
name: Food catalog search trust
description: Rules for turning external and curated search matches into safe, patient-visible foods
---

- **A text match or valid source record is not yet a loggable food.** **Why:** Open Food Facts can omit serving/macros, and official USDA files also deliberately include foods without nutrient amounts. Using a full-source archive as the loggable catalog either drops source evidence or invents zero-calorie foods. **How to apply:** preserve complete source snapshots independently of eligibility for patient search; keep the all-fields logging gate and promote reviewed, cited records rather than accepting incomplete upstream data.
- **Search failures use patient-safe copy, never transport details.** **Why:** generated client errors can contain an entire HTML 404 page such as `Cannot GET`, which is confusing and exposes internal routing. **How to apply:** sanitize catalog exceptions at the catalog client seam; HTTP prefixes, route diagnostics, and markup collapse to one short retry message while local saved-food results remain usable.
- **Reviewed aliases must win before the database applies its result limit.** **Why:** client/server presentation sorting cannot restore a friendly identity that the search RPC already omitted. **How to apply:** add exact aliases and their rank to the SQL search seam, then prove the intended source with an exact-query `limit=1` check; use presentation mapping only for labels and final tie-breaking.
- **Approved grouped-food direction:** Generic searches should show one recognizable food family, with lean/fat and preparation choices inside its details. **Why:** the user explicitly chose grouped results to avoid overwhelming patients with near-duplicate USDA descriptions and subsequently authorized implementation. **How to apply:** keep initial choices short and reveal meaningful variants in details; grouping is never approval to average distinct nutrition records.
- **Family grouping is presentation, not persistence identity.** **Why:** the small-choice experience must not replace an explicitly requested variation or rewrite a patient's historical nutrition. **How to apply:** explicit query constraints outrank remembered defaults; each selection keeps its precise source revision, portion evidence and original nutrient baseline, even when cross-source matches share one visible result.
- **Keep source baselines distinct from selected totals.** **Why:** a packaged product can summarize one serving while its complete nutrients use a per-100 basis; mixing these representations silently double-scales values even with passing types. **How to apply:** normalize provider IDs and nutrient units at one boundary, derive selected totals once, and check both rendered labels and saved payloads with a non-100-g packaged serving.
